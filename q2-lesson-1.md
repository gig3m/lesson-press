## For the Teacher

Read this before you teach. This quarter we're walking through the
Exodus story. Today is the setup: how God's people ended up enslaved
in Egypt, and what God did about it. The kids need to leave with one
idea firmly in place.

:::key-truth
God heard his people because he keeps his promises.
:::

**The covenant matters.** Back in Genesis, God promised Abraham that
his family would become a great nation and that through them, the whole
world would be blessed. By Exodus 1, Abraham has been dead for four
hundred years, his family is enslaved in a foreign country, and from
the outside it looks like the promise is dead. Exodus 2:24 is the verse
that breaks that tension: "God heard their groaning, and God remembered
his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob." God isn't
responding to suffering in general. He's keeping a specific promise
to specific people. That's the engine of the whole book.

**The four verbs in 2:24–25 are the heart of today's lesson.** God
*heard*, *remembered*, *saw*, and *knew*. The Hebrew piles them up on
purpose. After centuries of silence, the text floods you with God's
attention all at once. You want the kids to feel that shift.

**What we're skipping and why.** The full chapters include the Hebrew
midwives defying Pharaoh and Moses' birth and rescue. Those are coming
up in the next two weeks. Today stays focused on the question: did God
forget his people? Answer: no.

**Repeat the bottom line all morning.** Say it at least six times across
the lesson. Have the kids say it back. Write it on the board. End with
it.

:::materials
- Bibles for every student
- Whiteboard or large paper, markers
- Activity packet (printed in advance — see end of lesson)
- Pencils, crayons or colored pencils
- A basket
:::

## 1. Gather

Welcome the kids and have them sit together. Tell them you're starting
a new story today that will take several weeks to finish — the story
of how God rescued his people from slavery in Egypt.

:::ask
Has anyone ever had a promise made to them and then forgotten? How
did that feel?
:::

Let two or three kids share briefly. Then tell them: today we're going
to find out what happens when it looks like God forgot a promise — but
he didn't. We're going to learn one big idea, and they should listen
for it. Write it on the board:

:::say
God heard his people because he keeps his promises.
:::

## 2. The Story

### Setting the Stage

Before opening the Bible, the kids need to know where they are in the
larger story — don't assume they remember. Tell them, in your own
words:

:::say
A long time before today's story, God made a promise to a man named
Abraham. He told Abraham that his family would grow into a huge nation,
and that God would bless the whole world through them. Abraham had a
son, Isaac. Isaac had a son, Jacob. Jacob had twelve sons, and his
family moved to Egypt during a famine because his son Joseph was a
leader there. They were safe and well-fed.

But that was four hundred years ago. Four hundred years. Abraham is
long dead. Joseph is long dead. And now Abraham's family — which by
now is huge — is in serious trouble.
:::

### Reading the Text

Have everyone open to Exodus 1:8. Go around the circle and have each
student read one verse aloud through verse 14. If a student struggles
with a word, help them quietly. If a student doesn't want to read, let
them pass — don't make it a battle.

:::read
Exodus 1:8–14
:::

After the reading, work through these questions together:

:::discussion
1. The new king didn't know about Joseph. Why does that matter?

   *When leaders forget, people get hurt.*

2. Look at verse 12. What happens the more the Egyptians hurt them?

   *The Israelites multiply even more. God's promise to Abraham was
   that his family would be huge — even when Pharaoh tries to destroy
   them, the promise is still working.*

3. Verse 14 says their lives were "bitter." What's a bitter life?
   What does that feel like?

   *Hard work, no rest, no hope, treated like animals. Don't rush past
   this — the kids need to feel the weight before they hear the rescue.*
:::

:::transition
Now we're skipping ahead. There are parts of this story we'll come
back to in the next few weeks. Right now we want them to see what
happens after a long, long time of suffering.
:::

### The Four Verbs

Have them turn to Exodus 2:23. Pick a confident reader and have them
read Exodus 2:23–25 aloud, slowly.

:::read
Exodus 2:23–25
:::

Then tell the class to read those same verses again, in their heads
this time, and listen for four things God does. Give them thirty
seconds. Then have another student read the same verses out loud
again.

:::ask
What four things does God do in these verses?
:::

Write them on the board as the kids find them in their own Bibles:

> **God HEARD. God REMEMBERED. God SAW. God KNEW.**

Pause. Let it sit on the board.

### The Most Important Question

This is the hinge of the whole lesson. Slow down here.

:::discussion
1. Look at verse 24 in your Bible. What did God remember?

   *His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob. Make them
   find it themselves — don't say it for them.*

2. What's a covenant?

   *A serious promise, the kind you don't break. God made a covenant
   with Abraham four hundred years ago — and now, when his people are
   crying out, the Bible says God remembered it.*

3. Did God forget about his people during those four hundred years?

   *It felt like he forgot, but he didn't. God's timing isn't our
   timing. He was working even when his people couldn't see it. Let
   the kids wrestle with this — the honest answer is harder than it
   looks.*
:::

## 3. The Point

Say the bottom line again, with the kids:

:::say
God heard his people because he keeps his promises.
:::

Have a student look up and read Psalm 34:17 aloud:

:::scripture
Psalm 34:17

The righteous cry out, and the Lord hears them.
:::

:::say
The same God who heard his people in Egypt hears you. Not because
you're special on your own, but because of what kind of God he is —
a God who keeps his promises and pays attention to his people.
:::

### The Index Cards

Hand out index cards. Have each student write or draw one thing they
want God to hear from them — something they're sad about, scared of,
thankful for, or asking for. Tell them no one else will read it.
They'll fold it and put it in the basket, and you'll pray over the
basket.

:::note
While they write, walk around quietly. If a kid is stuck, help them
think of something specific.
:::

### The Memory Verse

When they're done, have everyone come back together and turn to Exodus
2:24. Have a student read the memory verse aloud:

:::scripture
Exodus 2:24

God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant.
:::

Then read it together three ways: all together normally, all together
in a whisper, and all together with one hand on their heart.

:::say
That word "remembered" is the most important word in the verse. God
didn't suddenly find out his people were hurting. He was keeping his
promise the whole time.
:::

### Closing Prayer

Hold the basket and pray:

:::prayer
God, you heard your people in Egypt because you keep your promises.
You hear these kids today for the same reason. Thank you for being a
God who doesn't forget. Help us remember, when we feel like you're
far away, that you are listening, seeing, knowing, and working — even
when we can't see it yet. Amen.
:::

## 4. The Work

Hand out the activity packet and crayons or colored pencils. The packet
has four parts the kids can move through at their own pace. They don't
have to finish all four — what matters is that they're working with
the lesson, not just killing time.

**Page 1 — Color and Remember.** A coloring page with the four words
from Exodus 2:24–25 (HEARD, REMEMBERED, SAW, KNEW) in big block
letters, with simple illustrations the kids can color. As they color,
they're staring at the four verbs.

**Page 2 — Word Search.** Words to find: ABRAHAM, ISAAC, JACOB,
COVENANT, PROMISE, EGYPT, HEARD, REMEMBERED, GROANING, MOSES (a teaser
for next week).

**Page 3 — Fill in the Blanks.** Five sentences from today's lesson
with one or two words missing. Example: *"God heard his people because
he keeps his ________."* The kids can use their Bibles or what they
remember.

**Page 4 — Draw It.** A blank box with the prompt: *Draw a picture of
a time you wanted God to hear you. He did.*

:::note
While they work, sit with them. Ask one or two kids what they're
drawing on page 4. Reinforce the bottom line one more time before
class ends. If you finish early, have a few kids share their word
search or what they drew. If you run long, the activity packet can go
home with them.
:::

## What Comes Next Week

Pharaoh's plan to kill the Hebrew baby boys, and the women — the
midwives, Moses' mother, his sister, even Pharaoh's own daughter —
who refuse to let it happen. God isn't just hearing his people. He's
already raising up the rescuer.
